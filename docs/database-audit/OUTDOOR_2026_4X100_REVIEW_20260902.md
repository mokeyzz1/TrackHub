# Outdoor 2026 4×100 reconciliation review

Date: 2026-09-02

Status: private audit complete; public repair remains intentionally disabled

## Scope

The existing `ingest.event_recovery_queue` contains 747 jobs for the bounded Outdoor 2026
window (2026-04-01 through 2026-06-30), one for every meet in that season window. The preparation
step reused those rows; it did not create a second queue or alter public result facts.

Of the 747 season meets, 350 had a public TFRRS URL for the first source comparison. A private
candidate pass then verified and audited 132 additional meets. The remaining 265 are blocked
because neither a public TFRRS URL nor a verified private candidate exists; they remain visible in
the private queue rather than being silently omitted, and are not guessed or classified as source
outcomes.

### Private source-candidate pass

A cached-index pass over the 397 initially blocked jobs page/date-verified 132 exact-name TFRRS
candidates. Those candidates were staged under the existing queue row's
`source_candidates.reconciliation.tfrrs_candidate` object only. Five names were ambiguous, six
failed page verification, and 254 had no exact cached-index match. The staged jobs were then
audited privately: 54 matched, 39 became repair-ready, and 39 need review. The 132 candidate
metadata objects remain preserved in the queue, and the 350 public `meets.tfrrs_url` links are
unchanged.

## First review batch

Start with the 237 broken-team actions across 55 meets. Every one of these local relay rows has a
NULL `team_id`; 104 are status-only marks such as DNS or DNF, and none are missing their meet link.
This is a contained local-integrity problem and can be reviewed without changing athlete or result
facts.

The unresolved-source-team bucket is broader: 744 actions across 145 meets and 346 distinct source
team names. It should be handled after the broken local rows, using the existing team catalog and
aliases rather than name-only inserts.

### Lineup-based candidates

An exact ordered-athlete-name comparison found 41 distinct broken local rows across 11 meets,
producing 45 candidate pairings. The guarded team-link plan ranks 24 as
`review_ready_exact_round`, 16 as `hold_round_confirmation`, and one as
`hold_team_not_canonical`. Forty-one pairings have equal marks (including normalized trailing `a`
suffixes); the remaining alternatives are preliminary/final conflicts that stay held. These are
source-backed review candidates only and remain unchanged.

The 16 rows with missing local round still require confirmation that the source Finals classification
is the correct historical round. They have no matching `ingest.observations` or
`ingest.source_links` provenance; their canonical relay rows were created in the August 6–7 import
batch with `round` unset, so the source round is comparison evidence but not a locally recoverable
historical field.

## Known truth-case check

The MIAA Outdoor Championships (`meet_id=12810`) still reproduces the expected discrepancy:
15 TFRRS rows, 6 local rows, 1 matched row, 14 missing rows, and 5 broken-team local rows. This
confirms the cleanup did not mask the underlying source/local difference.

## Safety and next gate

- No public result, relay, athlete, team, or meet row was changed by preparation or review.
- There is deliberately no public `apply` command.
- Production tracks 64 migrations; the five cleanup migrations applied directly are not yet
  represented in that history. Migration-history reconciliation must be planned separately and
  must not blindly re-run data migrations.

The next gate is manual/owner review of the 114 repair-ready plans and the 155 needs-review plans,
starting with unresolved teams and broken local team identities. Only after that review should a
separate, explicit public repair migration be designed.

## Exact-round structural checks

The six rows with exact local/source rounds were checked prospectively against the live schema:

- no existing relay-result uniqueness key would collide after the proposed team link;
- every proposed team ID is an active canonical `public.teams` row; and
- every relay has four non-orphaned athlete legs.

These checks establish structural safety only. They do not apply the links or authorize a public
migration; owner confirmation remains required.
